Pro Tips & Safety

Essential tips for better welds and critical safety guidelines for the virtual workshop.

Safety Guidelines

Eye Protection

Always wear proper welding helmet with correct shade lens (minimum #10 for MIG, #11 for TIG). Auto-darkening helmets recommended for convenience and safety.

Critical: Never weld without proper eye protection

Fire Prevention

Keep fire extinguisher within reach. Clear area of flammable materials. Sparks can travel up to 35 feet. Have a fire watch for hot work.

Critical: Hot slag can ignite surfaces 20+ feet away

Ventilation

Welding produces harmful fumes. Work in well-ventilated areas. Use fume extraction when welding galvanized steel, stainless, or painted materials.

Warning: Some materials release highly toxic fumes

PPE Requirements

Wear flame-resistant clothing, leather welding jacket or apron, welding gloves, and steel-toed boots. Remove synthetic fabrics that can melt.

Jacket Gloves Boots Cap

Electrical Safety

Inspect cables for damage before use. Keep work area dry. Never touch electrode while equipment is energized. Ground your workpiece properly.

Critical: Electric shock can be fatal

Cylinder Safety

Secure gas cylinders upright. Keep away from heat sources. Never drop or strike cylinders. Cap when not in use. Follow proper handling procedures.

Critical: Cylinders are pressure vessels - handle with extreme care

Pro Tips

1

Clean Metal = Good Welds

Always clean base metal with a wire brush or grinder. Remove rust, paint, oil, and dirt from the weld area. Contamination causes porosity and weak welds.

2

Tack Welding Strategy

Before running continuous welds, tack every 2-3 inches to hold pieces in position. This prevents warping and movement during welding.

3

Wire Stick-Out Rule

Maintain 1/4 to 3/8 inch wire stick-out (distance from contact tip to workpiece). Too short causes spatter, too long creates unstable arc.

4

Manage Heat with Technique

For thin materials, use stringer beads instead of weave. Pause briefly between short welds to let metal cool. This prevents burn-through.

5

Watch the Puddle

The weld puddle tells you everything. A healthy puddle flows smoothly and has slight ripples. Harsh popping means too much voltage. Hissing means contamination.

6

Gas Coverage Matters

Shielding gas protects the weld from atmospheric contamination. Keep gas diffuser clean and set proper flow rate (20-40 CFH for most applications).

7

Practice Foot Control

For TIG, use the pedal gradually. Slamming the pedal causes arc flash and spatter. Smooth, gradual adjustments produce consistent welds.

8

Post-Weld Inspection

Let welds cool naturally (no water quench). Inspect for cracks, porosity, and undercut. A good weld has uniform bead width and proper tie-in at edges.
