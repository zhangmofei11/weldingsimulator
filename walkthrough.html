Welding Simulator Walkthrough

A full progression route, from opening the shop for the first time to passing the expert blueprints with clean, repeatable beads.

Stage 1: Set Up the Workshop

Start on MIG

The MIG machine is the most forgiving rig in the game: continuous wire feed, built-in shielding gas and no foot pedal to manage. Learn the whole loop on MIG before you split your attention between processes.

Gear Up Before Arc Strike

Helmet, jacket, gloves and boots come before the torch. The safety training is part of progression, not flavour, and having the PPE on stops you losing time to fixes mid-job.

Read the Whole Blueprint

Check material type, thickness, dimensions and the marked inspection points before touching the controls. Material and thickness decide your settings; the inspection points decide where the score is won.

Your First Week in the Shop

Spend the first sessions repeating one joint type until your hand, not your head, is doing the work. Keep a note of the settings that produced a clean bead, because a welder who writes down 19V and 220 IPM welds the same part identically tomorrow, while one working from memory guesses.

Learn One Machine Deeply

Every rig has its own personality, and settings that worked on MIG will not transfer directly to TIG or Stick. Learn how the machine responds when you trigger and how the arc sounds at each end of its range. When you do change process, expect a fresh calibration period rather than a smooth transition.

Certification Tiers and What Each One Tests

Apprentice Journeyman Master Welder

The tiers below are a route through the blueprint list rather than a wall. Each one unlocks new machines and new materials, and every tier assumes the habits of the one below it. Pass a tier by completing its project twice: once to learn it, and once to prove you can repeat it on the same settings.

Apprentice

  • Flat bar butt joints in mild steel
  • Consistent travel speed on one axis
  • Clean metal, correct gap, no burn-through
  • Reads the puddle correctly

Journeyman

  • Tubes, pipe and multi-piece assemblies
  • Fit-up and tack placement that holds
  • Second process: TIG or Stick
  • No undercut or excessive spatter

Master Welder

  • Multi-pass heavy plate in Flux-Core
  • Aluminium and stainless TIG work
  • Distortion controlled by weld sequence
  • Inspection points pass without rework

Your First Weld, Step by Step

1

Prepare the Joint

Clean both faces back about an inch with a wire brush, then set the gap between the plates. Clamp or tack the pieces so they cannot move once the arc starts.

2

Set the Machine

For 1/8" steel start at 19-20V and 200-250 IPM with 75% argon and 25% CO2. Confirm the stick-out sits between 1/4 and 3/8 inch before you strike.

3

Strike and Hold the Arc

Start at the leading edge of the joint, hold the torch at 15-20 degrees from vertical and keep the arc length short. Watch for the puddle to form and flow in a smooth, rippling way.

4

Travel and Fill the Crater

Move at a constant speed all the way along the seam, then hold the torch in place and feed a touch more wire to fill the crater before you release the trigger.

5

Inspect and Reject

Let the bead cool, then run the inspection tools. A uniform bead width, clean root and no undercut means keep it. A cold-looking, spiky bead means lower the wire speed and repeat the pass.

6

Log and Move On

Note the settings that gave you a clean bead and reuse them on the next project of the same material. Once Flat Bar Joint is reliable, move to the Corner Bracket and keep your progress visible.

Mistakes That Stall New Players

Mistake Why It Costs You Fix
Changing everything at once You never learn which control fixed the weld Change one variable per attempt
Welding on dirty or rusty metal Guarantees porosity and a failed inspection Clean the joint area before every pass
Skipping the fit-up check Misaligned parts fail blueprint dimensions Measure and clamp before striking an arc
Forgetting to fill the crater Leaves a visible crack at the end of the bead Hold position and add wire on shutoff
Grinding the weld without cooling it You cannot judge the bead and you crack the root Let it cool naturally, then inspect and clean up

None of these mistakes are fixed by playing more in general. They are fixed by playing one job again with one change. Fix the biggest flaw, re-weld the same piece, and only move on to the next blueprint when the result looks the same twice in a row.
